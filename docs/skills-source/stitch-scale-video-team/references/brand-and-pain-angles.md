# Brand and Pain-Angle Reference

## Product identity

Stitch & Scale is a local-first knitwear grading and pattern-business workspace. Its current public job is to recruit founding testers, not to pretend that demand, revenue, or final pricing has already been proven.

The parent brand is Emlux. The founder is David Mokwunye, a developer whose late mother knitted. David does not knit and must never be described as a knitwear designer, pattern designer, or professional tech editor.

## Voice rules

Use first person, short declarative lines, quiet confidence, outsider wit, and genuine respect for craft. Sound like a builder asking designers to check his work. Lead with a craft truth before a technical explanation. Use concrete product proof and named limits. Avoid hype, countdown pressure, corporate language, or invented credentials.

Banned language includes: `streamline`, `empower`, `leverage`, `unlock`, `elevate`, `game-changer`, `revolutionary`, `AI-powered` unless literally true, `hustle`, and `scale your business` as a verb. Do not imply that the founder personally designs knitwear or that the product has proved commercial outcomes it has not proved.

Approved narrative territory includes: “I don't know how to knit. My late mother did. I wrote the math so you don't have to fight Excel at 11 PM.” The tester CTA is an invitation to join the founding cohort, try the demo, report real bugs, and help shape pricing and features. Do not use false scarcity or unverified discount claims; use the current approved tester policy only.

## Pain-angle ledger

Every video must choose one primary angle and one proof surface. Do not merge all angles into one feature tour.

| Angle slug | Human pain | Visual proof | Emotional turn |
|---|---|---|---|
| `xxl-lie` | Checkbox grading appears complete until larger sizes expose broken logic | One base size versus a transparent multi-size table; zoom one measurement family | The “complete” pattern was not complete; inspect the math before release |
| `spreadsheet-11pm` | Unpaid spreadsheet labor buries the care in handmade work | A late-night sheet or paper becomes an inspectable local-first workspace | The builder respects the hours designers do not invoice |
| `stale-number` | A changed measurement leaves yesterday’s number inside the instructions | Change one input and show a live placeholder or derived value update | A hidden stale number is more dangerous than an obvious error |
| `privacy-by-default` | Designers fear losing months of work or surrendering private patterns | Device-local project state and an explicit local-first explanation | The data remains in the designer’s hands while testers decide what comes next |
| `honest-price` | Designers price the object but not the hidden labor, fees, or yield | A transparent business-math receipt or cost chain | The pattern’s price should answer to the real work behind it |
| `test-knit-coverage` | A size can appear in a table without being tested by a real knitter | Test-knit roster across every graded size | Coverage is a production decision, not a decorative checkbox |
| `outsider-founder` | Designers distrust tools made by people who do not understand their craft | Founder disclosure followed by a direct request to audit the math | Outsider status becomes the reason to ask craft experts for help |
| `show-the-math` | “Trust us” software hides the decision path | One derivation, source, or visible warning at readable scale | Transparency is the product behavior, not a slogan |

## Concept-diversity test

Two concepts are not distinct if they reuse the same primary pain, first image, product proof, emotional turn, and final line. The Director must state the previous concept and name the changed five-part combination before approving a new brief.
