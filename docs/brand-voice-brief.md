# Brand Voice Brief — Stitch & Scale

*Prepared from public profile research + the founder's brainstorm archive (39 files), 15 Aug 2026. Living document.*

## Who the founder is (honest version — see Rule 1)

David Mokwunye is a 19-year-old indie developer. **He does not knit** — but his late mother did, and that is the genuine thread connecting him to the craft. He is the builder; the designers are the craftspersons. The public self-presentation comes from two artifacts: a terminal-style cover poster (`del limits.txt`, `breakthrough.exe`, `System status: UNSTOPPABLE`, "THIS USER IS GOING TO CHANGE THE WORLD") and a profile photo of an alien standing among humans in a crowded street. Together they say something coherent: a builder with defiant ambition who sees himself as an outsider quietly noticing what the crowd cannot — stated with a wink rather than a shout.

The parent brand is **Emlux**; Stitch & Scale is the product. His personal signature is **MOKDAVONY** (personal use only — never product branding).

## Rule 1 — The honesty rule (from the founder's own pitch playbook)

> Never sound like you're selling; sound like a person who built something useful and is asking for help making it better. The "I don't know how to knit" disclosure is a weapon, not a weakness — it disarms the exact skepticism every knitter feels toward a non-knitter tool.

Concretely: copy may **never** claim the founder knits or designs patterns. The authentic story is "a developer whose late mother knitted — I wrote the math so designers don't have to fight Excel at 11 PM." The testers' role is framed as the designers in the room verifying the math, which turns the founder's outsider status into the reason the tester cohort exists.

## The voice

| Dimension | Yes | Never |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Register | First person ("I built this because…") | Corporate "we streamline your workflow" |
| Rhythm | Short declarative lines; craft truth before tech truth | Marketing-speak, exclamation points as crutch |
| Metaphors | Terminals, code, systems — sparingly, as flavor | Jargon (ROI, synergy, leverage, empower) |
| Emotion | Quiet confidence + outsider wit + genuine love of the craft (via mother's legacy) | Hype, urgency gimmicks, countdown pressure |
| Credibility | Numbers and real product ("78 labs, 1,600 tests") + named limits ("I don't know yarn; you tell me") | Vague superlatives ("revolutionary", "game-changing") |

The brand tension that makes the copy work: **a hacker's hands building the business math for people with yarn on their hands.** The copy should always feel like a builder asking craftspersons to check his work — never a craftsperson lecturing.

## The tester-first funnel (current strategy)

The site's single job right now is to recruit **founding testers** — knitwear designers who will use the demo, say what they'd pay, and come back at launch. No paid spend until the early-access list proves demand. Everything on the page points to one of two actions: "try the demo" (instant, no signup) or "join the founding testers" (email). The founding-tester ask is framed as membership of a small first cohort whose feedback shapes pricing and features, not as a waitlist. **Testers who find real bugs get a significant discount when paid tiers ship** (founder promise, pitch playbook aligned).

## Anchors from the archive (keep copy consistent with)

The competitive wedge is **honest multi-size grading** — the market's own documented pain is "checkbox grading" ("Knitting's XXL Lie", 2026). The emotional through-line is invisible labor: hundreds of unpaid hours of spreadsheet math burying the care that goes into handmade work. The design posture is quiet, premium, craft-first: "Elegant. Quiet. Premium. Readable. Craft-focused."

## Copy seeds (approved direction)

Hero direction: craft truth first — e.g. "You can knit anything. But can you price it?" / "The pattern is only half the job. The math is the rest." Founder disclosure: "I don't know how to knit. My late mother did. I wrote the math so you don't have to fight Excel at 11 PM." Demo proof: "78 business labs. 1,600 tests. Free, no signup, your patterns stay on your machine." Founding-tester CTA: "Founding testers — the first cohort shapes what this becomes. Bug-hunters get a discount at launch. No spam, no fee, just honest answers."

## Banned terms

streamline, empower, leverage, unlock, elevate, game-changer, revolutionary, AI-powered (unless literally true), hustle, scale your business (as verb).

## Banned claims (new)

"Built by a knitwear designer" / any implication the founder knits or tech-edits professionally / invented credentials. The mother's knitting and the founder's code are the whole story — nothing more needs inventing.
