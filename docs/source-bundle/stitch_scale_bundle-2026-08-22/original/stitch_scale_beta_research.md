# Stitch & Scale beta research notes

## Official beta-stage guidance

GOV.UK Service Manual, How the beta phase works: https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/agile-delivery/how-the-beta-phase-works . It distinguishes private beta—invite a limited number of people to use the service in order to get feedback and improve it—from public beta, which opens the service to anyone who needs it once the team is confident it can run at scale.

Implication: Stitch & Scale should not begin with an unrestricted social-media blast. Use a private, capacity-limited beta first, then widen access after critical issues and support processes are under control.

## Official consent guidance

GOV.UK Service Manual, Getting informed consent for user research: https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/getting-users-consent-for-research . Participants should understand who is doing the research, its purpose, what data is collected, what will happen, how results will be used/shared, that participation is voluntary and can be withdrawn, how long data will be kept, rights and complaints, whether sessions are observed or recorded, and which organisations process the data. Researchers should keep evidence of consent and use/delete data within the consent given.

Implication: provide a plain-language tester information sheet and consent checkbox before collecting recordings, screenshots, testimonials, or identifiable feedback. Let beta use and public testimonial consent be separate choices. Keep the beta participation voluntary and explain that the software is unfinished.

## Social promotion guidance

Meta’s official Instagram creator update: https://about.fb.com/news/2024/10/best-practices-education-hub-creators-instagram/ . Instagram’s Professional Dashboard best-practices hub covers creation, engagement, reach, monetization, and guidelines, including posting frequency, attention, Reels length, trends/hashtags, engagement metrics, reach, and policy guidance.

Implication: use an Instagram professional account and its native insights to test content themes and audience response. Promotion should be educational and community-led, not only repeated product announcements.

## Participant privacy guidance

GOV.UK Service Manual, Managing user research data and participant privacy: https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/managing-user-research-data-participant-privacy . Research data should be deleted when no longer needed or when there is no clear consent. Public outputs should use fully anonymized extracts where possible; direct identifiers, contact details, and confidential information should be removed or obscured. Non-anonymized outputs should be restricted and not made public because copies are difficult to retract after consent withdrawal.

Implication: do not post tester screenshots, names, recordings, testimonials, or quoted feedback by default. Ask for separate, explicit permission for public use and anonymize by default.

## Testimonials, incentives, and social proof

FTC Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers . If a brand gives a person free or discounted products or other perks and the person mentions the product, the relationship should be disclosed even if the person was not explicitly asked to mention it.

FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule Q&A: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers . The rule took effect October 21, 2024. The FTC distinguishes consumer reviews from testimonials and notes that incentivized reviews can be testimonials. Do not condition an incentive on a positive review or ask testers for five-star ratings. Allow honest criticism, disclose any free access or compensation, and retain evidence of the arrangement.

Implication: beta testers may receive free access or a small thank-you, but the invitation should say that feedback can be positive, negative, or neutral. Public posts should disclose the tester relationship, and testimonials should never be edited to change meaning or selectively presented as proof that the product is error-free.
